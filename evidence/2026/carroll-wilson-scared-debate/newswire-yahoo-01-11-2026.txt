Jess Wilson has agreed to join a Leadership Forum against other key Victorian party leaders on one surprising condition. Picture: NewsWire
Victorian Premier Ben Carroll has agreed to Opposition Leader Jess Wilson's demand for a four-way leaders' debate.
"Of course I'll come to the (News 24) forum in three weeks, because I want to debate," he posted on social media on Saturday.
After weeks of refusing to participate in a three-way leaders debate which would include One Nation leader Warren Pickering, Ms Wilson agreed to the debate on the condition that the Greens leader was included as well.
Ms Wilson said she had "committed to a people's forum" between the leaders of the four major parties less than a month out from November 28 state election.
"We live in a democracy and ultimately it's Victorians who will decide this election. I'm pleased to be part of that four-way leaders forum," she said.
Jess Wilson backflipped after putting pressure on Victorian Premier Ben Carroll to debate her one-on-one. Picture: NewsWire/Ian Currie
Premier Ben Carroll has also agree to the four-leader debate after challenging Jess Wilson to debate him and the One Nation leader. Picture: NewsWire / Luis Enrique Ascui
Until now, Ms Wilson has refused to participate in any leadership debate outside the bounds of a traditional major party head-to-head between Labor and the Coalition.
"Ben Carroll has refused to debate me one-on-one, he's scared. He needs his comfort blankets in the Greens and One Nation in order to stand next to me and take questions from the people," she said previously.
"Ben, I've said many times before, I'm ready to debate you one-on-one … what are you scared of? Why won't you take me one-on-one and have a debate?"
Victorian Greens Leader Ellen Sandell will participate in the forum. Picture Newswire / Nicki Connolly
One Nation Victorian state leader Warren Pickering will also step up for the debate. Picture: NewsWire / Andrew Henshaw
While announcing her commitment to the leadership forum hosted by News24 and the Herald Sun, Ms Wilson was asked how she planned to debate Mr Pickering.
The Liberal leader, who has repeatedly been asked if she is considering forming a broader Coalition with One Nation, said she would attend the debate to talk about "my plan" and would "leave other candidates to them".
"It's Victorians who decide this election, it's Victorians who go into the ballot box and decide who they want to lead," she said.
The 90-minute forum hosted by News24 and the Herald Sun will be held on November 17 with the four leaders fielding questions from 100 swing voters.
The debate is on the eve of the start of early voting in the November 28 state election, which most opinion polls show Ms Wilson is in position to win.
According to the Herald Sun, the Greens and One Nation have both agreed to the terms of the four-way-debate debate.
