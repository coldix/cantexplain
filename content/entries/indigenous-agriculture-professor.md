September 2020. The University of Melbourne, a public university, [appoints](https://science.unimelb.edu.au/about/news/bruce-pascoe-appointed-enterprise-professor-in-indigenous-agriculture) Bruce Pascoe **Enterprise Professor in Indigenous Agriculture**. The job, the faculty said, was “designed to build knowledge and understanding of **Indigenous agriculture**.” [Country News](https://www.countrynews.com.au/news/bruce-pascoe-appointed-professor-of-indigenous-agriculture/): he was hired on the back of *Dark Emu*, “documenting and arguing for the restoration of forgotten food production.” The live staff page still lists him Honorary (Enterprise Professor), School of Agriculture.

This card files that chair. It does not file “no native plant should be studied.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- Nine months later the same university’s press published Sutton and Walshe, [*Farmers or Hunter-gatherers?*](https://theconversation.com/book-review-farmers-or-hunter-gatherers-the-dark-emu-debate-rigorously-critiques-bruce-pascoes-argument-161877). Their finding: not agriculturalists. Pascoe’s village-and-crop reading of the explorers does not survive the diaries. The chair had already been named.
- Pascoe is a writer. The appointment did not require an archaeology or anthropology doctorate. The title does the work the book did: agriculture as the settled fact, hunter-gatherer as the tag to drop.
- ABC Education, NSW Premier’s prizes, a Melbourne chair. That is the support. The second meter is the same campus’s own press.

Grammar: Indigenous Agriculture. Logic: a chair is an endorsement. Rhetoric: professor.
