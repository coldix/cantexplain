Pauline Hanson, Sky News, February 2026. The topic was Australian women and children in Syrian camps, the wives and children of Islamic State militants. She said she has no time for radical Islam. Then the line: “You say, ‘Well, there’s good Muslims out there.’ How can you tell me there are good Muslims?”

The [ABC headline](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-02-18/pauline-hanson-partially-apologises-for-muslim-remarks/106357130) filed it as “suggesting there are no ‘good’ Muslims.” Matt Canavan heard that slogan: no good people among Australian Muslims. The Senate later censured her on that reading. [Police, July 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-31/pauline-hanson-one-nation-hate-speech-police/106981544): it did not meet the hate-speech charge.

This card files the slide. It does not file “Hanson is a racist” and it does not file “there are no radical Islamists.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- Two days later she told the ABC she **did not believe** there are no good Muslims, and named a non-practising Muslim who stood for One Nation. The apology was for anyone who does not want sharia, multiple marriages, ISIS brides or a caliphate. Then: “In general, that is what they want — a world caliphate.”
- Asked by Chris Kenny whether the **overwhelming majority** of Australian Muslims are good people: “I don’t know that… When we’ve got **18,000** people on [the] ASIO watch list, how do I know who these people are?” The 18,000 is **her** figure. The [ABS, 2021](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/religious-affiliation-australia): Islam **813,400** people, **3.2%**. A watch list is not a census.
- “How can you tell” is a question about identification. “There are no good Muslims” is a verdict. The interview was about ISIS returnees. The slogan was about everyone.

Grammar: a question, in a specific interview. Logic: that is not a census. Rhetoric: the question mark fell off.
