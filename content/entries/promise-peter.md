Daniel Andrews, Channel 7, 28 November 2014, the night before the election. Peter Mitchell: will you promise not to increase taxes or introduce any new taxes? Andrews: “I make that promise, Peter, to every single Victorian.” Tim Pallas, the same week: Labor will not introduce any new taxes or increase any existing ones to fund its commitments.

By April 2017, [3AW](https://www.3aw.com.au/neil-mitchell-the-promise-daniel-andrews-broke-a-third-time-yesterday-20170430-gvw1sx/) had car stamp duty, some house-sale duty, and the Uber levy on the board. Andrews: not a broken promise. “We’ve delivered on each and every commitment we’ve made.”

This card files that sentence. It does not file “every tax is a rort.” COVID came later. The 2014 line did not have a pandemic clause.

**What did have a receipt:**

- [Victorian Auditor-General, AFR 2024–25](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/auditor-generals-report-annual-financial-report-state-victoria-2024-25/): the government “has introduced **several new taxes**.” Taxation income **+68.1%** since 2019–20, “driven in part by new taxes.” Mental health and wellbeing surcharge plus COVID-19 debt levies: **$3.1 billion** and **$3.3 billion** extra in 2023–24 and 2024–25. The COVID Debt Repayment Plan raised **$4.3 billion** through those levies. Taxes that year: **$38.9 billion**.
- [Windfall gains tax](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/windfall-gains-tax) — a levy when rezoning lifts land value. [Vacant residential land tax](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/vacant-residential-land-tax). Both sit on the State Revenue Office site. Both arrived after the Mitchell interview.
- Opposition lists count dozens of “new or increased” charges. Those lists are a campaign. The auditor’s figure, and the SRO pages, are not.

Grammar: no new taxes, no increases, every Victorian. Logic: a levy with a new name is still a tax. Rhetoric: “we’ve delivered on each and every commitment.”
