[ABC Education](https://www.abc.net.au/education/digibooks/bruce-pascoe-aboriginal-agriculture-technology-and-ingenuity/101734320), a classroom digibook for Years 5–10: “Bruce Pascoe states that Aboriginal people were the **world’s first bakers** and that they **invented bread**.” The same page asks whether grindstones mean flour **65,000 years** ago, and “Were they the world’s first bakers?” Pascoe has said it in his own voice: “I come from the country that **invented bread**. And that’s Australia.”

This card files that invention. It does not file “nobody ground seed on this continent.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- Seed-grinding is old in Australia. A grindstone is not a bakery, and processing seed is not planting a crop. Sutton and Walshe, in [*The Dark Emu Debate*](https://theconversation.com/book-review-farmers-or-hunter-gatherers-the-dark-emu-debate-rigorously-critiques-bruce-pascoes-argument-161877), separate hunter-gatherer processing from agriculture. Implements colonists called “picks” were not “for tilling or ploughing the soil in preparation for **planting seeds**.”
- The world’s earliest bread, on the usual archaeological definition, sits in the Natufian / Fertile Crescent record, millennia later than a grindstone and still not “invented in Australia.” ABC Education leaves the question mark in the heading and Pascoe’s statement in the lesson.
- The public broadcaster is the government in the classroom. The book had already won the NSW Premier’s prizes. The lesson plan is the support.

Grammar: invented bread. Logic: milling is not baking a first. Rhetoric: world’s first.
