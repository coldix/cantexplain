Julia Gillard, [16 August 2010](https://www.smh.com.au/national/pm-says-no-carbon-tax-under-her-govt-20100816-126ru.html), Network Ten, five days before the poll: “**There will be no carbon tax under the government I lead.**” The next day she told *The Australian* she ruled one out. The clip is on the official [PM transcripts](https://pmtranscripts.pmc.gov.au/release/transcript-17704). She does not deny saying it.

This card files that sentence. It does not file “Gillard is a liar” and it does not file “a price on carbon cannot work.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- [25 February 2011, 2GB](https://pmtranscripts.pmc.gov.au/release/transcript-17704), the day after she announced the mechanism: the first few years “would work **effectively like a tax**.” Later in the same interview: “There will a fixed price in the meantime. That is **effectively like a tax**.”
- [8 November 2011](https://pmtranscripts.pmc.gov.au/release/transcript-18251), after the Senate passed the Clean Energy package: “A fixed carbon price of **$23 a tonne** will apply from **1 July 2012**, moving to a flexible price after three years.” Combet, in the same release: “a charge on pollution, not a tax on households.” The charge was $23. The name was price.
- Parliament later repealed the package under its own title: *[Clean Energy Legislation (Carbon Tax Repeal) Act 2014](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2014A00083/latest/text)*. The repeal named the thing she had ruled out.

Grammar: no carbon tax. Logic: a fixed $23 charge is the noun she banned. Rhetoric: price, not a tax.
