Hera Sheppard, Victorian Socialists, ABC Radio South West Victoria, 12 August 2026. Asked about One Nation’s rise. She named Gina Rinehart, “pushing for Australian workers to work for two dollars a day.” The presenter did not contest it. Two days later the [ABC’s own corrections page](https://www.abc.net.au/news/corrections/2026-08-14/reinhartradio/107040362) called the line inaccurate and said an on-air correction had gone out.

This card files that slide. It does not file “Rinehart is a friend of the worker” and it does not file “the ABC is a conspiracy.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- [ABC, 5 September 2012](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2012-09-05/rinehart-says-aussie-workers-overpaid-unproductive/4243866), same masthead. Rinehart, Sydney Mining Club video: “Africans want to work, and its workers are willing to work for less than $2 per day. Such statistics make me worry for this country's future.” The lede on that page: Australians need to **compete with** African labour at that rate. West African iron ore, she said, was about **$100 a tonne** cheaper to bring on. That is a cost comparison. It is not a wage bid for Warrnambool.
- Julia Gillard answered the same day: “It’s not the Australian way to toss people a $2 gold coin.” She heard the implication in 2012. Sheppard filed it as a current Australian policy in 2026.
- The 2026 line also ties Rinehart’s money to One Nation’s poll rise. A donation is a donation. “$2 a day for Australian workers” is a different sentence.

Grammar: Africa, 2012, competitiveness. Logic: that is not “pay Australians two dollars.” Rhetoric: it went to air without contest. The correction arrived after the slogan.
