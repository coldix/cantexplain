The Torres Strait Regional Authority, on its [climate-change page](https://www.tsra.gov.au/the-torres-strait/climate-change/): climate change is an “**existential threat**,” and “most parts of the low-lying inhabited islands in the region **will be lost** to sea level rise.” The [Climate Council](https://www.climatecouncil.org.au/how-will-sea-level-rise-impact-your-city/) repeats the lost-islands line. BOM and CSIRO’s State of the Climate refrain is shorter: “**Sea levels are rising** around Australia.”

This card files that loss. It does not file “the sea is not rising.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- [CSIRO, State of the Climate — Oceans](https://www.csiro.au/en/research/environmental-impacts/climate-change/state-of-the-climate/oceans): global mean sea level up **just over 22 cm since 1900**, half of that since 1970. The [Climate Council](https://www.climatecouncil.org.au/how-will-sea-level-rise-impact-your-city/) writes the satellite rate as about **3.5 cm a decade**. A century of rise is still measured in centimetres.
- [Kench, Ford et al., *Nature Communications* 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-02954-1): all 101 islands of Tuvalu, four decades, local sea level up at about **3.9 mm a year** — twice the then-global average. Net land area **+73.5 ha (2.9%)**. Eight of nine atolls grew. **74%** of islands increased in size. The paper’s own line: results “challenge perceptions of island loss.”
- The same agencies already distinguish king-tide flooding from an island disappearing. Inundation of a graveyard is a receipt. “Will be lost” is a forecast that the atoll-area series has not been writing.

Grammar: will be lost. Logic: a 22-centimetre century is not a sinking. Rhetoric: existential.
