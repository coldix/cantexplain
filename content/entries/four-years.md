Malcolm Turnbull, 16 March 2017. Feasibility by the end of the year. Construction “soon after.” [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2017/mar/16/turnbull-2bn-snowy-hydro-electric-expansion): the project “would take **at least four years** to complete.” ARENA, quoted the same week, had already said pumped hydro can take **up to seven**.

December 2017, the study landed. [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2017-12-21/snowy-hydro-2.0-could-actually-cost-as-much-as-$4.5b/9277368): “operational **from 2024**.”

This card files those dates. It does not file “never finish.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- [ABC, 3 June 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-03/billions-over-budget-years-late-how-snowy-hydro-2-got-here/106706518): **seven years** behind the first schedule. First power now late **2027**. All units: **December 2028**. That is eleven years from the announcement, four past the 2024 study date.
- [ANAO, June 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-18/auditors-say-snowy-2-0-not-holding-contractors-accountable/106700336): there is “**no agreed baseline schedule**.” The auditor could not say how far off completion the job was. Productivity gains assumed at the 2023 reset “are proving difficult to realise.”
- Four years was a floor. 2024 was a year. A schedule the auditor cannot find is a third clock.

Grammar: at least four, from 2024. Logic: a floor is not a finish. Rhetoric: soon after.
