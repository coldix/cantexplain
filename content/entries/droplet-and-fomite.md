[Medical Journal of Australia](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2020/consensus-statement-safe-airway-society-principles-airway-management-and-tracheal), 16 March 2020. Safe Airway Society consensus, for people who put tubes in throats. The community line came with it: transmission is “**primarily through droplet and fomite**.” Drops fall in “**a few seconds**,” “**short distances**.” Aerosols? Plausible under certain conditions, “**not the primary mechanism**… in the community.”

This card files that primary. It does not file “they knew and hid it.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- That sentence is why Australia wiped benches and stood on crosses. [1.5 m to stop the spread](https://cantexplain.au/hall/one-point-five) is this paper, sent as an SMS.
- Choirs, restaurants, poorly ventilated rooms did not behave like spit that hits the floor in a few seconds. WHO later treated airborne spread as real. The tape on the supermarket floor did not come up.
- “Not the primary mechanism” is a hedge. The policy treated it as a no.

Grammar: droplet, fomite, few seconds. Logic: hanging indoor air is not a bench. Rhetoric: primarily.
