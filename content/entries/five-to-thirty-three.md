1 July 2004, the 2003 Zoning Plan comes in. [Reef Authority timeline](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/50-years/timeline): the plan aimed to “**protect and conserve the biodiversity** of the Great Barrier Reef ecosystem.” Green zones — no-take — from **five per cent** of the Marine Park to **33**. Queensland mirrored it in state water. Twenty-one thousand submissions. The largest no-take network on the planet, sold as the thing that would keep the Reef.

The Outlook still lists “[remaining impacts from fishing](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/news/great-barrier-reef-outlook-report-2024-ecosystem-under-pressure)” next to climate, mud and starfish. Plibersek, 2024: “**sustainable fishing**” as one of the three pillars that kept UNESCO’s “in danger” stamp in the drawer. Then a **$185 million** gillnet phase-out.

This card files that expansion. It does not file “open every green zone.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- Fish biomass in no-take zones is a real meter. Hard coral cover is another. [AIMS](https://www.aims.gov.au/monitoring-great-barrier-reef/gbr-condition-summary-2024-25) still writes the cover years as bleach, cyclone, crown-of-thorns. Yasi 2011, Hamish 2009, Debbie 2017 — after the 33%. The central Reef hit its LTMP floor **seven years** into the new zoning.
- [De’ath 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1208909109): the 50% loss split is storms, starfish, bleach. Fishing is not a column.
- Gillnets kill dugong and turtle. That is a different animal to Acropora. The zoning slogan treated “protect the Reef” as one verb.

Grammar: protect, conserve, five to 33. Logic: a no-take line is not a cyclone track. Rhetoric: world’s most comprehensive.
