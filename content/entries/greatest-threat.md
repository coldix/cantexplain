The Reef Authority’s refrain. It sits under [reef-health update after reef-health update](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/learn/reef-health/reef-health-updates), including after the [August 2026 AIMS report](https://www.aims.gov.au/monitoring-great-barrier-reef/gbr-condition-summary-2025-26) that cover was rising again: “**Climate change remains the greatest threat**… urgent global action.” Greatest is not “a threat.” Greatest is a ranking.

This card files that ranking. It does not file “the sea did not warm.” 2024’s bleach was real, and AIMS said it was the main killer **that** summer.

**What did have a receipt:**

- [De’ath et al., *PNAS* 2012](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1208909109) — the government’s 50% paper — ranked the losses: cyclones **48%**, crown-of-thorns **42%**, bleaching **10%**. Yasi in 2011 put the central Reef at its LTMP floor.
- [Peter Ridd, Senate](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Rural_and_Regional_Affairs_and_Transport/GreatBarrierReef/Report/section?id=committees%2Freportsen%2F024386%2F73345): “**major cyclone events**” primarily responsible for the cover declines in the monitored series. AIMS 2025–26 still names **Koji** and **Narelle** in the same summer as the recovery.
- AIMS’s own 2024–25 line is honest: bleach was the primary mortality **that year**, with cyclones and starfish in the mix. “Greatest threat” is the line that does not move when the cover does.

Grammar: remains, greatest. Logic: a ranking that never updates is a slogan. Rhetoric: urgent global action.
