Nikole Hannah-Jones, [The 1619 Project](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2019/08/14/magazine/black-history-american-democracy.html), August 2019: “Our founding ideals of liberty and equality were **false when they were written**.” The Pulitzer Center turned the issue into a free [K-12 curriculum](https://pulitzercenter.org/sites/default/files/full_issue_of_the_1619_project.pdf). By May 2020 it was claiming **4,500 classrooms**. The lead essay won the 2020 Pulitzer Prize for Commentary.

This card files that sentence. It does not file “slavery was a footnote.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- Leslie M. Harris, the historian the *Times* asked to fact-check, [wrote in Politico](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/i-helped-fact-check-the-1619-project-the-times-ign) that she “**vigorously disputed**” the claim the patriots fought the Revolution “in large part to preserve slavery.” The paper ran it anyway. She listened, she said, “in stunned silence” as Hannah-Jones repeated it on air.
- The same essay tells students one “critical reason” the colonists declared independence was “to **protect the institution of slavery**.” Harris: protection of slavery “was not one of the main reasons the 13 Colonies went to war.” The Revolutionary War, she wrote, became a “primary *disrupter*” of slavery in the North.
- The *Times* later issued a clarification on the Revolution passage. The classroom PDF kept travelling. The slogan on the cover did not change: the founding ideals were **false**.

Grammar: were false. Logic: a fact-checker is not a decoration. Rhetoric: 1619 as founding.
