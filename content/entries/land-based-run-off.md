The Reef Authority, [water-quality threat page](https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/learn/threats/declining-water-quality). Position statement, 17 February 2021: “Poor water quality is a **major threat**.” The pollutants named are farms: fine sediment, excess nutrients, pesticides. Plibersek, [25 July 2024](https://minister.dcceew.gov.au/plibersek/media-releases/joint-media-release-great-barrier-reef-world-heritage-status-retained-labor-governments): **$200 million** more for gully work and grazing, so UNESCO would not write “in danger.”

This card files that ranking. It does not file “mud never hurt a coral.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- [Senate, Ridd and Piers Larcombe](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Rural_and_Regional_Affairs_and_Transport/GreatBarrierReef/Report/section?id=committees%2Freportsen%2F024386%2F73345): cyclone resuspension of seabed mud is “**far more destructive** to the Reef than agricultural practices.” The Consensus Statement, they said, did not treat that as the larger pulse.
- Same inquiry: crown-of-thorns at **Swains**, **150 km** offshore, “completely unaffected by runoff from the land.” A native starfish in plague numbers before European farms. The nutrient-from-cane story does not reach that reef.
- The Authority’s own sentence is already hedged: “**inshore** areas.” The slogan travels as the whole Reef. AIMS’s cover highs in 2022 and the 2026 bounce are mostly mid- and outer-shelf, where the river is not the weather.

Grammar: greatest contributor, inshore, agricultural. Logic: a flood plume is not a cyclone’s 30 cm of stirred mud. Rhetoric: major threat.
