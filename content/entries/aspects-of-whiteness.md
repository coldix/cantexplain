The Smithsonian’s National Museum of African American History and Culture put a chart on its [Talking About Race](https://nmaahc.si.edu/learn/talking-about-race/topics/whiteness) site for educators: “**Aspects and Assumptions of Whiteness** in the United States.” [Newsweek, 17 July 2020](https://www.newsweek.com/smithsonian-race-guidelines-rational-thinking-hard-work-are-white-values-1518333): the list included “**objective, rational, linear thinking**,” “**hard work** before play,” delayed gratification, the nuclear family, individualism. “White dominant culture, or **whiteness**,” the page said, has been “normalized” as the American standard.

This card files that chart. It does not file “the museum never documented racism.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- The museum [removed the graphic](https://nmaahc.si.edu/learn/talking-about-race/topics/whiteness) within days. Statement: it did “not contribute to the productive discussion we had intended.” The **Whiteness** page stayed. Robin DiAngelo stayed on it.
- The chart was adapted from Judith Katz’s 1978 anti-racism training handbook — the same pipeline that put *White Fragility* into staff days and *Racism. No Way!*’s [white-privilege](https://cantexplain.au/hall/white-privilege) lesson into Australian Years 9–10.
- Hard work and the scientific method are not a race. A federal museum listed them as one.

Grammar: aspects of whiteness. Logic: a virtue with a colour code is a slogan. Rhetoric: talking about race.
