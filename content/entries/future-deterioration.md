The Reef Authority, 23 August 2024. Fourth Outlook Report. [Official news page](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/news/great-barrier-reef-outlook-report-2024-ecosystem-under-pressure): the overall outlook “remains one of **future deterioration** due largely to climate change.” Same paragraph: coral habitats and seagrass “**have improved**” over the five-year window. The arrow and the bounce sat on one page.

This card files that arrow. It does not file “the water is not warming.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- [AIMS, 4 August 2022](https://www.aims.gov.au/information-centre/news-and-stories/highest-coral-cover-central-northern-reef-36-years): northern and central hard coral cover at the **highest** since monitoring began — **36%** and **33%**. Northern had been **13%** in 2017 after cyclones, crown-of-thorns and the 2016 bleach. That is a recovery, not a eulogy.
- [AIMS 2024–25](https://www.aims.gov.au/monitoring-great-barrier-reef/gbr-condition-summary-2024-25): the Reef had “**record high** coral cover” immediately before the 2024 bleach. Then the largest annual drops in the north and south in 39 years — down toward the long-term average, not to zero. Three reefs of 124 under 10%.
- [AIMS 2025–26](https://www.aims.gov.au/monitoring-great-barrier-reef/gbr-condition-summary-2025-26), 11 August 2026: cover in all three regions “increased slightly or remained similar.” Northern **35.1%**, above a long-term average of **27%**. Central **31.6%**, above **20%**. “Start of recovery.” The Outlook’s future was already a past bounce.

Grammar: remains, future, deterioration. Logic: a record high is not a corpse. Rhetoric: overall outlook.
