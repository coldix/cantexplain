Bruce Pascoe, [*Dark Emu*](https://www.magabala.com/products/dark-emu), 2014: reconsider the “**hunter-gatherer** tag.” The 2018 subtitle went further — *Aboriginal Australia and the Birth of Agriculture*. [ABC Education](https://www.abc.net.au/education/digibooks/bruce-pascoe-aboriginal-agriculture-technology-and-ingenuity/101734320) put it in front of Years 5–10: the book “challenged the belief that the First Australians were hunter-gatherers” and taught “**vast agricultural fields**.” The NSW Premier’s Literary Awards made it Book of the Year *and* Indigenous Writer’s Prize, 2016. That is a government stamp.

This card files that tag. It does not file “Aboriginal people did not manage country” and it does not file “Pascoe invented every grindstone.”

**What did have a receipt:**

- Peter Sutton and Keryn Walshe, [*Farmers or Hunter-gatherers?*](https://theconversation.com/book-review-farmers-or-hunter-gatherers-the-dark-emu-debate-rigorously-critiques-bruce-pascoes-argument-161877) (Melbourne University Press, 2021): classical Aboriginal people were “highly successful **hunter-gatherers and fishers**.” They “strongly repudiate” the idea that this was primitive. They find the agriculture thesis does not hold. Pascoe’s edit of Sturt’s “village” of **19 huts** is “**misleading at best**.” Sturt’s diary: people did not live in those houses all year.
- The same ABC classroom resource, after the fact, adds a note that Sutton and Walshe “evaluated differently.” The years of assigned reading came first. The note is a footnote.

Grammar: hunter-gatherer tag. Logic: a tag you drop still needs a crop. Rhetoric: birth of agriculture.
